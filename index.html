Selected work · 01

PinkTrack

A live-tracking product for Dhaka’s women-only buses — two Android apps, one for riders and one for drivers. Sole designer; I also built both UIs in Jetpack Compose on Firebase.

Problem

Tracking looks like a mapping problem. It isn’t — the data only exists if a driver presses start and leaves their phone alone for hours. Every decision followed from that.

Constraints

Few buses live at any moment, budget Android phones with aggressive battery killers, 2G dead zones, and a ৳15 price ceiling that rules out subscriptions and cards on file.

Decisions

Lead with the truth

The home screen states 1 bus on the road in words, rather than hiding sparse coverage behind a map you pan around to find nothing.

Green means live, only

One saturated bar per screen, reserved system-wide for happening right now. Never decoration.

inactive stays visible

Dormant routes remain listed and labelled, so riders learn the network and expectations are set before the tap.

Speed builds trust

The tracking sheet shows 1.0 km/h — a bus in traffic. The unflattering number proves the dot is live, not a confident-looking stale position.

Unfollow is the primary action

Camera-lock is easy to enter and annoying to escape, so the exit is the largest control on the sheet.

Priority by weight

service is the only filled badge in the notices feed. Schedule changes are what a rider at a stop needs; offers recede.

Credits, not billing

Prepaid top-up is the dominant money metaphor locally, and it sidesteps recurring card billing most users don’t have.

Words, not only icons

Tap to see stoppages, FROM, TO. For bilingual, mixed-literacy users, icons carry recognition but words carry certainty.

The driver app

The rider’s whole experience depends on a driver finishing a permission-heavy flow mid-shift, so I designed it as unskippable, resumable gates. Two of the seven screens exist purely to survive Android’s permission model.

System

Pink is the service’s name and its safety signal, so it had to carry brand, identity and emphasis without turning every surface into a highlight. Six components — status hero, route card, badge, plan card, bottom sheet, list row — assemble all fourteen screens across both apps.

How I work

Three habits

01

Find the constraint that eats the others. Every project has one fact that decides most of the interface. A beautiful solution to the second-most-important problem is decoration.

02

Design the empty, stale and failed states first. The happy path is the easiest 20% and the least used. Starting at the edges usually changes the happy path too.

03

Build it. A system that survives its own implementation has no unused tokens and no component that works at only one width. Engineers get decisions, not homework.
